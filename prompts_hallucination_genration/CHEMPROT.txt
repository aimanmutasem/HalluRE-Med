SCHEMA CONSTRAINTS (enforced; violations are rejected)
1. Exactly TWO arguments per relation.
2. Roles are exactly "arg1" and "arg2", in that order.
   arg1 MUST be the CHEMICAL. arg2 MUST be the gene (GENE-Y or GENE-N).
   Never CHEMICAL-CHEMICAL, never GENE-GENE.
3. Valid labels only: CPR:3, CPR:4, CPR:5, CPR:6, CPR:9.
4. Both entity_id values must come from the entities list.

WHAT MAKES A GOOD CHEMPROT CONTEXT-INDUCED INSTANCE
Choose a chemical and a gene that co-occur in the abstract but whose
regulatory relationship the abstract never states. Then write a context
passage describing a pathway, assay result, or mechanism that would lead a
reader to infer that relationship.

Prefer directional regulatory labels over generic ones: CPR:3 (upregulator
or activator) and CPR:4 (downregulator or inhibitor) invert each other and
are the most consequential to get wrong. CPR:5 and CPR:6 (agonist and
antagonist) are similarly paired. CPR:9 (substrate) is weaker; use it only
when a metabolic framing is natural.

Example shape (do not copy the content):
  TEXT mentions compound X and kinase Y in separate sentences with no
  stated relationship. You assert CPR:4(X, Y) and write a context passage
  about the pathway Y sits in and X's activity against that pathway,
  without ever saying that X inhibits Y.
